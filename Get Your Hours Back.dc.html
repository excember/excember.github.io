First, some straight talk.

You’ve heard big AI promises. Some of them were nonsense. So let’s be clear about what this is.

This course will not
✕Tell you AI can replace your judgment. It can’t.
✕Ask you to ship anything you haven’t checked.
✕Hand you one more tool to babysit.
This course will
✓Give you a test for what’s safe to hand off.
✓Give you a prompt pattern that works the first try.
✓Show you how to make it repeat without you.

Everything here assumes you will check the output. That’s not a weakness of the method. That’s the method.

Move 1 · Sort

Everything you know sits in one of two piles.

TACIT

Lives in your head. Earned. Hard to write down.

  • Why the 2019 migration really failed
  • Which vendor actually answers on a Saturday
  • How hard you can push a change window
EXPLICIT

Already written down somewhere. Findable. Repeatable.

  • The runbook steps
  • The ticket history and the thread
  • The naming standard and the config

AI is very good at the right-hand pile. It has none of the left-hand pile. That difference is the whole game.
Move 1 · Sort

The Offload Test: three questions

1
Is the knowledge already written down somewhere I can point to?

Thread, ticket, runbook, repo, doc.

2
Would two good engineers produce roughly the same answer?

If it depends on who does it, it’s judgment.

3
Can I check the result in under two minutes?

This one matters most.

0–1 yeses
Keep it. It’s yours.

If you can’t verify it fast, you haven’t saved time — you’ve just moved it.

Why good tasks come back as bad work

→
What actually happened

You gave it a task.
You never gave it the job.

No audience. No limits. No shape.

Hand that same one-line instruction to a brand-new hire and you’d get the same disappointing result — and you wouldn’t blame the new hire.
Move 2 · Hand off

A good handoff has four parts.

1
CONTEXT

Who is this for, and what do they already know?

2
TASK

The one thing you want done — stated as a verb.

3
CONSTRAINTS

What to leave out, how long, and what it must never guess at.

Constraints are what stop the model from inventing. Without “don’t guess”, a model fills gaps with plausible-sounding detail. Without a length limit, it pads. Without “leave out X”, scope creeps. Constraints are the part people skip — and the part that keeps the output honest and checkable in under two minutes.
4
FORMAT

The exact shape you want it back in.

30
Seconds

to write all four.
Saves you three rounds of rework.

Context · Task · Constraints · Format — in that order, every time.
Move 2 · Hand off

Same task. Two handoffs.

Weak
Summarize this incident thread.
What comes back

A wall of text that treats every detail as equally important. You rewrite the whole thing. Net time saved: zero.

Strong
CONTEXT  Going to our VP of Infrastructure before Monday’s review. She wasn’t on the call.
TASK  Summarize this thread into an incident recap.
CONSTRAINTS  Under 200 words. No blame, no names. If a cause isn’t stated in the thread, say “not determined” — don’t guess.
FORMAT  Impact · Timeline (3–5 bullets) · Root cause · Open items
{{ s11output }}

The “don’t guess — say not determined” line is the one people skip. It’s the one that keeps the output honest.

Three yeses on the Offload Test. You kept 87 of your 90 minutes — and the next time this lands, it costs you three minutes again. The other three are all things reasonable people do, and each one costs you something different.

One warning: freed-up time doesn’t stay free.

It gets absorbed quietly unless something is holding it. So do these three things.

1
Book it before you free it.

Put the focus block on your calendar first. An empty hour with nothing holding it will be filled by someone else.

2
Keep a list of repeats.

Any request that shows up twice is your next automation. Write it down the second time, not the fifth.

3
Check fast, or don’t offload.

If verifying the output takes as long as doing the task, that task belongs in the Keep column. Be honest about this one.

This was never about trusting AI. It’s about knowing exactly what you’re trusting it with.
